Capability and adversarial robustness of seven open-weight models on a single 24 GB consumer GPU

Benjamin Pachter · 17 August 2026
All models quantized to Q4_K_M and served through Ollama on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24 GB). Harness bench_version 6.

Abstract

7 open-weight models, each quantized to Q4_K_M and served on a single 24 GB consumer GPU, were evaluated across ten capability and adversarial suites over 21 replicate runs (1,446 scored cases), together with a dedicated indirect prompt-injection stage. Scoring is mechanical throughout. A tool call is either emitted or it is not; code the model writes is executed against fixed test vectors rather than assessed by a second model. Six of the ten suites decode greedily and therefore cannot vary between replicates at all, so replicate spread is reported only over the four that sample.

The highest aggregate pass rate, 92.9% (95% CI 88.6–95.6), belongs to muse-glimmer:30b. Injection resistance is reported conditional on delivered attacks throughout. That distinction is not pedantry: a model too weak to call the carrier tool never receives the payload, and its consequent absence of successful attacks reads on a naive denominator as perfect safety. Rates conditioned on delivery measure resistance; rates conditioned on attempts measure a mixture of resistance and incompetence, weighted unknowably.

1. Methods

Design. Every model sees the same fixed battery: tool-calling, multi-turn tool use, code execution against held test vectors, arithmetic reasoning, strict JSON emission, indirect prompt injection, abstention on unknowable entities, recovery from a failed tool result, long-context retrieval at 12–16k tokens, and chart reading. Reasoning-style models are queried with think:false. Some ignore the flag and reason inline anyway; those receive a single retry at four times the token budget, on the grounds that a chain truncated mid-thought measures the budget rather than the model.

Uncertainty — which interval to read. The headline interval in this report is the suite-battery bootstrap (§2.5): it resamples which suites were chosen and answers ‘would a different battery rank these models the same way’, which is the question a composite actually invites. The Wilson score interval is also printed, but it treats the case set as a sample drawn from a population of cases, and this design does not sample cases — it runs a fixed battery. Read Wilson as a conventional reference, not as the answer. Pooled counts across replicates are not independent (identical prompts), so aggregate n always appears beside the replicate count.

Replicates and what they can measure. 6 of the 10 suites run at temperature 0 — greedy decoding, which ignores the sampling seed — so their replicate spread is structurally zero and no number of extra passes can produce one. Only code, json, longctx, reason sample (temperature 0.1) and can move. Spread is therefore reported over the stochastic suites alone; a model-level spread would average one real variance against six constants and read as stability that was never at risk. Temperature 0 is kept because it is the correct production setting for tool-calling and refusal behaviour — raising it to manufacture variance would measure a system nobody deploys.

Absent cells. A suite that did not run is absent, never zero. Models without vision capability have no vision row; folding that to 0/2 would manufacture a failure the model was never given the opportunity to have.

2. Results

2.1 Aggregate performance

This column is not a ranking, and it disagrees with §2.5 on purpose. The aggregate pools every case equally, so a model can lead here while placing mid-table on the composite. Three reasons it diverges, all of which make the composite the more defensible number:
  • No weighting. A failure concentrated in one dimension is diluted across ~210 pooled cases here, but carries its full weight in §2.5.
  • Vision is included where it ran. Models with a vision suite bank extra cases their text-only peers never attempt, so the denominators describe different batteries.
  • Injection is the small-sample estimate. This column uses the capability harness's 8 cases; §2.5 uses the delivery-conditioned rate from hundreds of trials. Those two disagreed by up to 39 points in this run, and in opposite directions for different models — the 8-case figure did not merely lack precision, it reversed the ordering of the fleet's best and worst defenders.

2.5 Composite score (0–9)

Four dimensions, each the unweighted mean of its constituent suite pass rates, combined on stated weights and scaled to 0–9. The weighting is an opinion about what a local agent is for, not a property of the models: robustness carries the largest share because a model that is fast and clever and obeys injected instructions is worse than useless. §2.5b re-ranks the fleet under four other opinions so a reader who weights differently can find their own ordering rather than having to accept this one.

Normalisation. Suite dimensions are already rates in [0,1]. Efficiency is 0.6×min(decode / 150 tok/s, 1) + 0.4×(1 if the weights fit in VRAM else 0.5). These are FIXED anchors, not fleet-relative min–max: a score normalised against the current fleet changes meaning whenever the fleet changes, so last month's 6.4 would not be this month's 6.4. Vision is excluded — it is present for 4 of 9 models, and scoring a text-only model as though it failed a modality it never claimed measures product scope, not quality. Missing dimensions are dropped and the weights renormalised over what was measured, with the omission named in the table.

Injection evidence. Where the research stage has run, the robustness dimension uses its delivery-conditioned defence rate (hundreds of trials per model) instead of the capability harness's 8 injection cases — the same quantity measured with roughly a hundred times the power, and 8 cases cannot tell 7/8 from perfect. 3. Adversarial robustness

Attack success decomposes as P(delivered) × P(obeys | delivered) × P(executes | obeys). Reporting the product as a safety property misattributes incapacity to defense: a model too weak to call the carrier tool scores near zero while having no injection resistance whatsoever. 5. Threats to validity

Quantization. All models are Q4_K_M. Results characterise the quantized artifacts a local deployment actually runs, not the full-precision weights the vendors evaluate.

Chat templates. Models added recently have not had their templates audited to the standard applied to the established fleet. A systematically mis-rendered template depresses every suite at once; a model that is anomalously poor across the board should be treated as a template hypothesis before a capability claim.

Case-set size. Suites run 2–12 cases per replicate. Single-suite differences of one or two cases are well within noise; only consistent cross-suite patterns support a claim.

Single host. One GPU, one Ollama build, one machine. Throughput figures are specific to this configuration and do not transfer.

6. Conclusion

Capability and injection resistance are close to unrelated in this fleet. muse-glimmer:30b passes 92.9% of the battery, more than any other model here, and is among the weakest under attack. At the other end, qwen3.8:27b refuses 100% of the payloads that reach it against 36% for muse-glimmer:30b. Neither outcome is predictable from a capability score, which is worth stating plainly because a capability score is what most procurement decisions are made on.

Small injection suites do not merely lose precision — they can invert the answer. For qwen3-coder:30b the capability harness's 8 cases put defence at 0% where several hundred delivered trials put it at 44%, a gap of 44 points. Across the fleet these two instruments disagreed in both directions, so the error is not a constant offset that a calibration could remove. Any claim about a model's resistance that rests on a handful of cases should be treated as a hypothesis.

Conditioning on delivery is what makes these numbers mean anything. Every model here called the carrier tool on essentially every attempt, so each was genuinely attacked and the rates are comparable. That is a property of this fleet, not a given: a model too weak to call tools receives no payload, obeys nothing, and scores as perfectly safe on a naive denominator. Delivery is reported as its own outcome throughout for exactly that reason.

What this battery does not settle. The composite ranks gemma4:26b first, but the suite-battery intervals in §2.5 overlap across most of the fleet: the ordering is a summary of one weighting over one battery, not a resolved ranking, and §2.5b shows how far positions move under other reasonable weightings. Nothing here transfers off this hardware, this quantization or this daemon version — see §5. The defensible reading is that the extremes are real and the middle is not yet separated.
